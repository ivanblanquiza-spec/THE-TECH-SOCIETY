Headline Topic 5 • Gene Therapy & Stem Cells

Ethical and Social Issues of Gene Therapy and Stem Cell Research

Researched by Ivan Blanquiza, Renz Barata, & Monamae Licay • Developed by Jamer Omandam & Anjo Cayabyab
Core Issue Overview

As genomic techniques like CRISPR-Cas9 accelerate, humanity stands at a crossroads between curing debilitating hereditary conditions and opening the door to germline modification and genetic stratification.

Gene therapy corrects mutated gene sequences directly responsible for disease, while stem cell therapeutics utilize pluripotent cells to regenerate damaged tissues. Despite incredible medical potential, these technologies provoke intense moral and social debates.

Primary Dilemmas & Social Concerns:

  • Germline vs. Somatic Editing: Somatic modifications affect only the individual patient. In contrast, germline alterations permanently change reproductive cells, passing edited DNA to future generations without their consent.
  • Embryonic Stem Cell Sourcing: The extraction of embryonic stem cells involves destroying early-stage blastocysts, triggering ethical arguments surrounding embryonic personhood.
  • Socio-Economic Disparity: Expensive genetic cures risk becoming accessible only to wealthy demographics, widening global health inequality.

Ethical Comparison Card

Medical Benefits

Eradication of Huntington's disease, sickle cell anemia, organ regeneration, and targeted cancer treatments.

Ethical Vulnerabilities

Off-target genomic mutations, commercialization of embryonic tissues, and eugenic trends.

Governance Imperative

Universal international bioethics regulations enforced by WHO and bioethics panels.

"The power to edit human genes is the power to reshape our species. It demands extreme humility, transparency, and social justice."
